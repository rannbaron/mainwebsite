\noindent \textbf{\large Math 105L} \hfill \textbf{\large Lab 4 Part 2}\\[1pt]
{\textbf{\Large Human Growth: Rates of Change, Acceleration, \& Concavity}}\\[-5pt]
\rule{\textwidth}{1.2pt}

\vspace{0.03in}

\textbf{\large 1. Background: Zoomed Data on Ages 8--14 Years (95.5 to 160.5 Months)}

In this lab session, we examine the relationship between the graph of stature (height $H(t)$ in cm) and the graph of rate of growth ($H'(t)$ in cm/year):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, topsep=1pt, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Height function $H(t)$:} Represents the median stature of children at age $t$ (in months).
  \item \textbf{Growth rate $H'(t)$:} Computed as $\frac{\Delta H}{\Delta t} = \frac{H(t+\Delta t) - H(t)}{1/12 \text{ yr}}$, giving growth speed in cm/year.
  \item \textbf{Growth Acceleration $H''(t)$:} The rate of change of the growth rate, measured in $\text{cm}/\text{year}^2$.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.03in}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=boxbg, colframe=boxframe, boxrule=0.8pt, arc=3pt, left=8pt, right=8pt, top=4pt, bottom=4pt]
\textbf{\large The Core Mathematical Connections: Shape of $H(t)$ vs. Behavior of $H'(t)$}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, topsep=2pt, itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{Growth Spurt Peaks (Maximum of $H'(t)$):}
  \begin{itemize}[label=--]
    \item When the rate curve reaches a local maximum, the child is growing at their fastest possible rate.
    \item On the height graph $H(t)$, this corresponds to the point of \textbf{maximum steepness}.
    \item This point is an \textbf{inflection point} of the height curve where concavity switches from up to down.
  \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Growth Rate Valleys (Minimum of $H'(t)$):}
  \begin{itemize}[label=--]
    \item When the rate curve reaches a local minimum, the growth has slowed to its minimum rate before the adolescent growth spurt begins.
    \item On the height graph $H(t)$, this corresponds to the point of \textbf{least steepness}.
    \item This point is also an \textbf{inflection point} where concavity switches from down to up.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

\vspace{0.04in}

\textbf{\large 2. Male vs. Female Growth Comparison}

From the classroom data projection:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, topsep=1pt, itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Female Adolescent Growth Spurt Peak:}
    Occurs at approximately \textbf{140 months} ($\approx 11.7$ years). At this age, the female height curve is steepest, after which growth decelerates and the curve bends downward (concave down) toward adult stature.
  \item \textbf{Male Adolescent Growth Spurt Peak:}
    Occurs later, at approximately \textbf{160+ months} ($\approx 13.3+$ years). Male peak growth rate is higher and occurs roughly 1.5--2 years later than the female peak.
  \item \textbf{Pre-Spurt Minima (Troughs):}
    Occur at $\approx 115$ months for females and $\approx 125$ months for males.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.04in}

\textbf{\large 3. Acceleration and Concavity Formalization}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=chalkblue!6, colframe=chalkblue, boxrule=1pt, arc=3pt, left=8pt, right=8pt, top=4pt, bottom=4pt]
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, topsep=1pt, itemsep=3pt]
  \item \textbf{Acceleration $> 0$ ($H''(t) > 0$):} The growth rate $H'(t)$ is increasing. The height graph is \textbf{concave up} (bending upward).
  \item \textbf{Acceleration $< 0$ ($H''(t) < 0$):} The growth rate $H'(t)$ is decreasing. The height graph is \textbf{concave down} (bending downward).
  \item \textbf{Inflection Point of $H(t)$:} Occurs wherever acceleration passes through zero ($H''(t) = 0$ with a sign change), corresponding exactly to the peaks and troughs of the rate curve!
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}
