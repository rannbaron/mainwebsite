Math 105L: Video Project

One of your major bits of work for the semester will be to create a video explaining a mathematical concept, either one from our class, or any other bit of math you find interesting or are curious about. This sheet outlines the project, the timeline for it, its structure, and its assessment.

Organization

You will work in groups of four or three (most groups will be three). All students must appear in the video, and all students must do approximately equal work on topic selection and the storyboard/script (see below). Your video can be longish (up to eight minutes) or short (but no less than 2.5 minutes) - the key is to clearly explain a concept!. My suggestion would be to use TikTok. However, you may use any software you like.

Content

The video you write, storyboard, and produce will introduce or explain a mathematical concept to a calculus student audience. You may pick a calculus topic or something outside calculus. Some ideas are:

105L Concepts Others
What is a Limit? Zeno’s Paradoxes
Finding Tangent Lines Fractals
Using Log Rules to Solve Equations Tessellations
Continuity and Differentiability The Möbius strip and Klein Bottle
Using the Product and Quotient Rules Non-Euclidean Geometry
The Chain Rule The history of zero
Transforming Functions The Abacus
What does the slope of a curve mean? The Fibonacci Sequence and the Golden Ratio
What concavity tells us about human growth Hotel Infinity
Proving the Power Rule

You do not have to choose from this list – any mathematical topic you come up with may work! Just check your idea with me!

Your video can be serious or silly. It can be spoken or sung. It can use a whiteboard or paper, or take place outside. It can be about how you understood a concept that started off being really hard, or explain another interesting bit of math.

Structure

You will produce two major bits of work for the project: A storyboard and script (click that link to find out what a storyboard is!); and the video itself.

Timeline

  • You will need to meet and research a topic by Monday, Nov 2. On that day, your group should send me an email with your choice of topic and a list of resources you plan to use to learn about it. I will reply within a day or two, either asking for a bit more detail (or more resources), or approving your decision.s
  • The storyboard and script for your video will be due Friday, Nov 13 at 8am. I would be more than happy to meet to discuss your work once in between these dates. It would be a good idea for your group to plan to have such a meeting with me. I will grade and return the storyboard and script to you within a week. I may ask you to make revisions. If so, those will be due Wednesday, Nov 18
  • Your final video will be presented to the class during Week 15, the week after Thanksgiving. Your group will be assigned a slot to present in, on Monday or Friday of Week 15.

Assessment

You will need to pass both the storyboard and script assignment and the final video assignment in order to getting a passing grade for this element of the class:

  • Storyboard Your storyboard must include a slide for each scene of your video, explaining what will be seen and heard.

  • Script Your script should include all text spoken (or sung!) for each scene. The script must clearly explain the mathematical concept you choose. 50% of the script must pertain directly to the mathematical concept you choose. The script must be mathematically accurate.

  • Video You will get judged on the audio/visual clarity and quality; group participation (all members must be participating); and matching the script/storyboard.
